Recomeço Verde

A green new beginning — a rehabilitation facility built with the forest, under the intact canopy of Príncipe's UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.

The problem

Príncipe has no lawful detention capacity

An autonomous region should have its own justice infrastructure — and Príncipe’s leaders are right to want it. Today, the region’s autonomy stops at the courtroom door. Its only detention capacity is two 48-hour holding cells at the Comando Geral — rain-flooded, unsecured, and pressed into open-ended preventative detention. The receiving room where women report domestic violence shares a wall with those cells, and victims walk past the holding yard to reach it. Everyone sentenced is shipped across the sea to São Tomé — severed from family, community, and any path back. The conventional answer on the table is a USD 2.5–4 million concrete compound that would clear 7,816 m² of primary Biosphere forest. It is unfundable, oversized for a caseload of 10–15 prisoners a year, and indefensible in the one place on Earth whose brand is its forest.

Recomeço Verde proposes the opposite: a low-security, rehabilitation-centred facility elevated on fully reversible screw-piles beneath the canopy — the same methodology as Príncipe's award-winning Sundy Praia eco-lodge — where residents learn cacao, vanilla and spice production tied to the island's premium value chains, and leave with seedlings, tools, and a standing buyer. This is not the alternative to a facility — it is how Príncipe actually gets one: fundable, buildable, and the first of its kind anywhere. The facility that completes the region’s autonomy also puts it at the front line of global corrections practice.

Life under the canopy

An eco-lodge that happens to be secure

Locked lodges at night, supervised movement by day, physical male/female separation — security through design, dignity by default. No bars. No barbed wire. No towers. Admission is by individual risk assessment, and on a 139 km² island with one airstrip and one port, the sea itself is the perimeter. Today, reporting abuse on Príncipe means walking past the accused. Recomeço Verde ends that.

Where residents live

Six modular five-bed lodges under intact canopy — bolt locks and storm shutters at night, a 10,000 L rainwater tank, and greywater feeding the banana circles.

Where skills become livelihoods

The curing atelier: vanilla graded into cedar conditioning boxes, cacao fermented to export standard — the productive heart of the facility.

A working farm under the canopy

Shade-grown forastero cacao, hand-pollinated vanilla, pepper, cinnamon and coffee — between and around the structures, never instead of the forest.

Off-grid by design

Solar and battery in a natural canopy light-well; rain harvested into twin 10,000 L tanks with gravity sand filtration.

The loop closes here

The Viveiro Recomeço nursery and composting bays — where every graduate's exit-package seedlings are raised.

Where the change happens

The counselling treehouse: classroom and one-on-one counselling high in the cooler air, a full solar roof, and vanilla vines climbing the very stilts that hold it up.

Shared with the community

The red-sand pitch: solar drying yard in the morning, the village football ground in the afternoon.

Security without walls

The living fence: a thorn-thicket barrier, ironwood tutor trees carrying vanilla vines, and a discreet ankle-height sensor line.

Why partner

A narrative donors want to fund

Something genuinely groundbreaking: a heartfelt, productive institution that fuses rehabilitation, environmental restoration, respect for the Biosphere, and private-sector engagement — on the island best placed on Earth to prove it works.

A working contribution to the Biosphere

Not merely compatible — the facility actively restores: agroforestry over degraded patches, a native-species nursery, environmental education, zero-carbon operations. The Natural Dividend logic, applied to justice — direct synergy with the Faya Foundation's USD 17.6M programme.

Proven on this island

HBD's Sundy Praia eco-lodge pioneered the exact screw-pile, under-canopy methodology — the architectural precedent stands 20 minutes away.

Completes the justice chain

The final link in the PBF digital justice chain: from rights awareness to post-release reintegration — a first for a small island developing state.

Commercial, not charity

Claudio Corallo and Diogo Vaz buy premium output; HBD anchors eco-tourism. Private-sector engagement on commercial terms.

For the Regional Authority: Recomeço Verde completes Príncipe’s institutional autonomy — justice served on the island, families intact, sentences that end in skills rather than severance — and gives the region a facility no other jurisdiction on Earth can show. Delegations will come to Príncipe to study it.
Why 30 beds: 10–15 admissions a year across multi-year sentences means 25–35 residents at steady state — and reporting is rising as trust builds, with the DireitosAoPovo rights platform about to amplify it further. There is no overflow on Príncipe; the outer limit is the design limit. The site itself sits in the hills above Porto Real — noticeably cooler than Santo António, a natural advantage for residents, staff and curing sheds alike.
Status: preliminary concept for discussion with the Ministry of Justice and the Regional Autonomous Authority of Príncipe. It does not represent a commitment by UNODC or any partner organisation; partnership references are indicative. Cost estimates require validation through detailed design.
